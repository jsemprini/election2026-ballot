    How the numbers are built

    Definitions

    % Yes
    Yes ÷ (Yes + No) when vote counts are available, otherwise the reported percentage. Values marked ≈ are approximate; measures reported only as “Majority Yes” have no share.
    Yes position
    The workbook records which ideological side won each measure. Combined with the result, that gives the side a Yes vote took (e.g., a measure that failed with a “Liberal” win has a conservative Yes). Measures coded “Mixed” (bonds, institutional changes) appear on the measures map but not in ideological comparisons.
    Gap
    Vote share for the selected position minus the same-side party’s candidate share (Republican for conservative positions, Democratic for liberal ones), in percentage points. Positive = the position ran ahead of the candidate; negative = it ran behind. A state’s value is the unweighted mean across its measures.
    Two-party share
    Candidate share of the two-party vote, D ÷ (D + R). Ballot measures are two-way choices, so this is the closer comparison; “Raw” uses the reported share including minor candidates.

    Caveats

    • Results are unofficial and incomplete — the workbook covers a subset of the measures on 2026 ballots.
    • Runner-up candidates are assumed to be from the other major party.
    • Alaska’s Governor race uses partial, first-round ranked-choice shares, which inflates its gaps.
    • South Carolina’s Senate shares are imputed from the reported margin.
    • Iowa Amendment 1 is cited in the workbook’s footnote (Passed ~56%) but has no data row; it is included and flagged.
